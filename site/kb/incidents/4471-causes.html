#4471 · what the merge hid

owner support desk accepted reviewed 2026-10-31 · 0 days ago

#4471 was closed as one bug, “dates parse mm/dd in eu locales”, with one fix queued. Sorted by cause, the reports merged into it are two bugs. They break dates at different points on the way from the booking form to the calendar, so they need different fixes, and only one of them had a ticket.

The desk merges on the subject line (decision 0001). Both bugs arrive under the same subjects, so the merge could not see the split, and the smaller bug never got a ticket of its own.

The two problems

parser — the shared date parser reads every date month first. A tenant that writes the day first gets its dates flipped: 7 April comes out as 4 July. This is what #4471 describes, and it is the bug with the queued fix.

hint — booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. A tenant that writes the day first follows the hint and types the month first, and the form then reads that input the tenant’s way. Nothing is wrong with the shared parser here: the hint asks for one order and the form reads the other. The tenants on booking-v3 produce these reports, and only through the form. Same subject lines as the bug above, different bug, different fix.

What the desk did with them

Engineering closed #4471 on 5 October. The parser fix shipped, fix(dates): parse dd/mm for eu locales. The second bug was closed as won’t fix, because booking-v3 is retired: the form that asks for one date order and reads the other no longer runs. Both tickets in the repo are closed the same way.

How the reports were counted

The export repeats some rows: the ticket system’s pages overlap, so a report can arrive twice. One id is one report, so the count starts from unique ids, not from rows.

The cause of a report comes from its tenant, not from what intake tagged it. A tenant has a locale and a booking form (src/lib/data/tenants/), and causeOf in src/lib/kb/causes.ts combines those with the channel the report came in on. Intake’s meta.intakeTag is a guess taken from the reporter’s browser, and it disagrees with the cause for a large share of these reports; the counts here ignore it.

Reports the desk read as a one-day move stay counted where they were filed, and are footnoted under the table below.

Lessons

  • A merge on subject lines cannot split a ticket by cause. The cause lives on the tenant and the channel, so check it before a ticket is closed as one fix.
  • Two bugs that look alike to the reporter can need two fixes. Count them separately before engineering plans the work.
